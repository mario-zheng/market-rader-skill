**AI Voice unlocks the “dark data” in calls, and provides new ways to engage with customers in the**

strong from telecoms operators in “AI in every call” - as evidenced at MWC26 by Deutsche
Telekom and LG Plus and others (see Telco Network AI Voice section). AI Voice presents new
category opportunities for Telecoms and Intelligent Engagement players.


●​ **Market Potential** . Current total “Conversational AI” market estimates are order of magnitude
~$10bn. AI Voice sits within this, and in value pools exceeding $300bn across customer care,
CRM and digital engagement. Even modest participation in these adjacent pools implies
revenue potential measured in $ billions / tens of billions. We anticipate new market data and
forecasts in 2026 as analysts catch up with the rate of change of the technologies.


●​ **Recent Market Signals.** Announcements at Enterprise Connect 2026 further illustrate the
acceleration of AI Voice within communications platforms. Vendors including RingCentral and
Sinch demonstrated AI agents embedded directly into communications infrastructure,
enabling enterprises to deploy conversational automation across voice, messaging and digital
channels.

mainstream in advanced service organisations, save time (often 30%+) and improve quality.
Customer evidenced benefits include facilitating scaling, first-time resolution, improving
automation containment, and cost reduction. AI Voice demonstrates lead conversion and
improved booking rates uplift up to 3X, and growing ability to address basic transactions.
Compliance monitoring can extend from sampling to 100% of interactions. (See business

●​ **Analytics are less mature in adoption** but combined with effective governance and
automation tools and processes (e.g. vCons) show significant potential to deliver value.

understood, governed and achieved. In effect, AI Voice shifts communications economics
from transport pricing toward the control of data, intelligence and execution. **The strategic**

**orchestrates the data flows in the conversation**, and therefore who captures the value.


●​ **Intelligent Engagement players** (CPaaS, CCaaS, UCaaS +) are part of an ecosystem including
hyperscalers, telcos and IT players, many of whom are fighting for the prize of a stake in this
market.

**KEY ACTIONS FOR INTELLIGENT ENGAGEMENT PLAYERS**


_Source: CPaaSAA_


1.​ **Co-create the narrative: position yourself as an Intelligent Engagement player.**
CPaaS/CCaaS/UCaaS tags do not intuitively convey the ability to support customers with AI
Voice or any other intelligence based engagement. If you continue to use this language, you
will become further marginalised in customers’ minds. You need to drive Intelligent
Engagement and describe yourself as an Intelligent Engagement player. Position AI Voice
within Intelligent Engagement, refine a common narrative structure to describe component
propositions, and support with clear outcome focused case studies.


2.​ **Evidence business results.** Move the conversation from technology features to business
results. Focus on benefits of unlocking scale, reducing handling time, improving conversion
and scale economics. Product strategy should address budget scrutiny and ROI directly. Shift
pricing towards outcomes based when feasible.


3.​ **Arm your partners** (and educate your/their customers). AI Voice is a new and fast developing
field, and sales teams that aren’t incentivised and aren’t ready to deliver concise, compelling
explanations will certainly not be able to sell it. Equally, customers need to know “what can I
do with AI?” If you don’t help them, someone else will.


4.​ **Engage early with emerging regulation.** AI Voice may sit at the boundary between
AI-generated “content” and electronic communications services. The way regulators classify
these interactions could materially affect how services are designed, disclosed and governed.
Intelligent Engagement players should actively engage in emerging regulatory processes –
including the EU AI Act transparency Code of Practice – to ensure that real-time
communications use cases are properly understood and that workable standards for
disclosure, marking and governance are developed.

5.​ **Control orchestration (the runtime layer).** The orchestration layer governs routing,
escalation, policy and model execution. Control here determines who captures intelligence
margin and who remains a transport provider. Focus your messaging and product design to
show customers the strengths that partnering with you as an orchestrator delivers (i.e. points

**AI Voice and Conversational Intelligence**

It’s helpful to put AI Voice in the context of some other terms used in the industry, because some of
the words and phrases sound similar but have nuanced meanings:


●​ **Intelligent Engagement**, discussed above, refers to _customers’ desired end-goal outcome_ of
being able to interact cohesively with their ecosystem in real-time


●​ **Conversational Intelligence** describes the _process of bringing together different channels of_
_communication_ (e.g. calls, emails, texts, etc.) _and applying systematic intelligence_ to those
communications that treats them as part of an ongoing conversation with the customer


●​ **Programmatic Communications** is a definition that arose to describe how _a class of earlier_
_stage media_ (i.e. such as text, emails and IVRs) could be automated using IT


●​ **Enriched Engagement Media** describes _the newer class of media or channel_ (e.g. RCS,
WhatsApp, AI Voice) which are designed to be both _richer in format and more interactive and_
_flexible in function_


AI Voice sits at the intersection of Enriched Engagement, Conversational Intelligence, and Traditional
Voice, as AI Voice can also augment person-to-person voice (e.g. through transcription, translation,
agent co-pilot, etc.)

●​ **Intelligent Engagement** is the overarching concept, the business outcome of acting with

**ETHICS, PRIVACY AND REGULATORY COMPLIANCE**


The rapid evolution of AI Voice capabilities, especially in biometrics, sentiment analysis, and the use
of large language models, introduces critical legal and ethical considerations that impact
implementation at the enterprise level.


Compliance with evolving data privacy regulations such as GDPR, CCPA, and new legislation
governing the use of biometric identifiers and voice recordings is a fundamental prerequisite.


Enterprises must establish robust governance frameworks to ensure transparent data usage, secure
customer consent for voice data processing (particularly for biometric authentication), and protect
against misuse, such as deepfake vulnerabilities.


Risks from unintended consequences or failures of AI use include:


●​ **Hallucinations causing compliance breaches.** AI models can generate plausible but
factually incorrect outputs. In regulated environments such as financial services or healthcare,
an AI agent acting on or communicating a hallucinated response could constitute a
compliance violation, create legal liability, or cause direct customer harm.


●​ **Data retention and consent missteps.** Voice data captured for AI processing (transcription,
analysis, biometrics) may be stored, shared or used in ways that inadvertently breach GDPR,
CCPA or sector-specific regulations. Consent frameworks designed for traditional call
recording may not adequately cover the broader uses enabled by AI.


●​ **Cost blowouts from transcription and inference.** AI Voice workloads are
consumption-based, and costs can scale unexpectedly with call volume, model complexity
or real-time processing requirements. Without robust usage monitoring and budget controls,
enterprise deployments can exceed projected costs significantly.


●​ **Vendor lock-in via embedded copilots.** As AI copilots become deeply integrated into agent
workflows and CRM systems, switching costs rise sharply. Enterprises that build processes
around a specific vendor's AI tooling may find themselves structurally dependent on that
vendor's pricing, roadmap and data access terms.


●​ **Model drift.** AI models can degrade in performance over time as the real-world language,
topics and customer behaviours they encounter diverge from their training data. Without
ongoing monitoring, a model that performed well at deployment may quietly become less
accurate or appropriate, without any obvious failure signal.


●​ **Evaluation gaps** . Many organisations lack the frameworks, metrics or tooling to assess
whether their AI Voice deployments are actually performing as intended. Without rigorous
evaluation, underperformance, bias or unexpected behaviour may go undetected until it
causes a visible problem.


For -aaS and CSPs, supporting customers with compliant, 'privacy-by-design' solutions is a
non-negotiable step to reduce adoption friction and build trust.


These risks do not prevent adoption, but they mean governance must be designed into AI Voice
deployments from the start.

**THE FOUR VALUE LAYERS OF AI VOICE**


_Where value sits: AI Voice economics are shaped by four layers ranging from infrastructure to_
_outcomes._


AI Voice reorganises where value sits in live enterprise conversations. The economic centre of gravity
is moving between the following layers beyond Transport.


1.​ **Transport & Usage** . Minutes, tokens and media streaming remain the foundation. Telephony
APIs, routing and AI processing are typically billed on consumption. This layer is essential, but
exposed to price pressure and efficiency gains that can reduce billable volume.


2.​ **Intelligence** . Analytics, copilots, sentiment detection, biometrics and compliance monitoring
make voice computable. Revenue may still be metered per minute or API call, but the value
driver shifts to interpretation, risk management and quality improvement. Differentiation
expands at this layer.


3.​ **Outcomes** . AI Voice increasingly links to operational performance: call containment,
conversion uplift, reduced handling time, improved compliance. Pricing models extend
beyond usage to seats, workflows or enterprise contracts. Budget ownership can move from
telecoms to operations, customer experience or revenue functions.


4.​ **Structured Conversation Data** . When conversations are captured, structured and governed,
for example through vCons, they become reusable assets. Structured data strengthens
analytics, supports audit, improves model performance and embeds the platform deeper into
enterprise systems. This layer increases switching costs and strategic leverage.


**Strategic implications**


●​ The largest long-term margin pools sit above transport, and Intelligence revenues are likely to
scale more sustainably than transport and usage


●​ Outcome-based pricing reshapes budget conversations, and ownership of structured
conversation data strengthens defensibility. Efficiency may reduce minutes, but intelligence
increases value per interaction.


●​ Control of runtime determines who captures value across these layers


The opportunity for Intelligent Engagement players is to compete in the added value layers.

**Why it matters where the intelligence is orchestrated in Telco Network AI Voice**


In Telco Network AI Voice, the operator always runs the call, and determines how AI might be
triggered (*87 etc.), the price, terms and conditions etc, and the AI processing, e.g. ASR, translation
and TTS may sit on cloud infrastructure in any scenario.


Strategically, it still matters where the intelligence orchestration layer sits - that is, the runtime that
decides which models are used, how calls are handled, where data flows, how policies are enforced
and when escalation occurs.


If that orchestration sits in e.g. a hyperscalers’ domain, AI in the network becomes a feature layered
onto connectivity. The telco controls access and billing, but the execution logic depends on that
hyperscaler. Costs remain variable. Switching suppliers requires re-integration. Innovation follows the
hyperscalers’ roadmap.


If orchestration is embedded within IMS, media servers or edge environments, even if external AI
models are used as components, the operator controls execution. Models become interchangeable
inputs rather than architectural anchors. The telco can manage the cost curve, negotiate from a
stronger position with suppliers, and design new services without restructuring the stack each time.


Either way, embedding AI in the call path improves the offer, but controlling the orchestration layer
changes the economics and the power balance.


T-Mobile’s careful silence on who they’ve partnered with for their eye-catching beta suggests that
they are mindful of this trade-off. It will be interesting to see what approaches other telcos take as the
space develops.

subsequently.

## CONCLUSIONS


_Strategic takeaway: AI Voice will redistribute value in communications toward players who_
_control intelligence, orchestration and enterprise integration._


AI Voice creates a new opportunity within Intelligent Engagement. It turns live conversation into
computable, governable and monetisable interaction. Intelligent Engagement is the business framing:
the shift from fragmented communications toward coordinated, real-time action across customers,
partners and workforce. Runtime is the control point within that shift. It determines how conversations
execute, how intelligence is applied, how compliance is enforced and where structured data
accumulates. The strategic question is therefore not simply who provides AI, but who governs
execution, and who captures the value that follows.


**INTELLIGENT ENGAGEMENT PLAYERS IN AN AI VOICE WORLD**


AI reduces the economic importance of how long people talk, and increases the economic
importance of what is understood and done.


AI Voice opens new fields for Intelligent Engagement players. It can be monetised as a per-minute or
per-token service, and/or as a platform offering priced per seat, per workflow, or at enterprise level.
The pricing model will depend on customer need, application maturity, and market context.


However, while AI may reduce the number of minutes per call, it does not reduce the value of the
interaction. In many cases it increases it. That value now depends less on duration and more on the
success of the intelligence deployed. The paradigm is shifting from transport-driven value to
intelligence-driven value.


Differentiation therefore lies not in access to models alone, but in bridging the gap between
theoretical AI capability and operational business reality. Customers need governed execution,
integration with enterprise systems, compliance assurance, quality control, and reliable outcomes.
This is where Intelligent Engagement players can create defensible advantage.


How to harness AI effectively is now a board-level question. AI Voice is one of the most immediate
and visible arenas in which that question must be answered. The opportunity for Intelligent
Engagement players is to provide solutions that are more operationally complete and commercially
accountable than hyperscaler models alone.


Enterprise platforms such as Salesforce are also embedding conversational AI into CRM
environments, reinforcing that the future control point for AI Voice will sit across communications,
cloud and enterprise software platforms.


**As conversations become computable, the profit pool shifts toward those who orchestrate the**
**intelligence that runs them.**


Because intelligence now drives value, the control point moves from pure connectivity to
orchestration. If communications platforms remain at the transport layer, value will migrate upward in
the stack. If they own the runtime (the execution environment that governs models, policy, integration
and escalation) they remain central to enterprise conversation strategy.


This section outlines what will shape their success and what they must do now.

**STRATEGIC IMPERATIVES FOR AI VOICE SUCCESS**


**Frame the Market Narrative / Value Proposition**


**1. Co-create the narrative: be an Intelligent Engagement player.** Talk less about technology and
more about the outcomes ( _Intelligent Engagement_ ). The story requires good structure (e.g., Automate,
Assist, and Analyze) and strong case studies to bring value to life.


**2. Evidence business results.** Move the conversation from technology features to business results.
Focus on benefits of unlocking scale, reducing handling time, improving conversion and scale
economics. Product strategy should address budget scrutiny and ROI directly. Shift pricing towards
outcomes based when feasible.


**3. Arm your partners** (and educate your/their customers). AI Voice is a new and fast developing field,
and sales teams that aren’t incentivised and aren’t ready to deliver concise, compelling explanations
will certainly not be able to sell it. Equally, customers need to know “what can I do with AI?” If you
don’t help them, someone else will.


**4. Engage early with emerging regulation.** AI Voice may sit at the boundary between AI-generated
“content” and electronic communications services. The way regulators classify these interactions
could materially affect how services are designed, disclosed and governed. Intelligent Engagement
players should actively engage in emerging regulatory processes – including the EU AI Act
transparency Code of Practice – to ensure that real-time communications use cases are properly
understood and that workable standards for disclosure, marking and governance are developed.


**Secure Platform Control & Champion Governance (Technology & Operations)**


**5. Control orchestration (runtime).** The orchestration layer governs routing, escalation, policy and
model execution. Control here determines who captures intelligence margin and who remains a
transport provider. Focus your messaging and product design to show customers the strengths that
partnering with you as an orchestrator delivers (i.e. points 6-11 below). Communications platforms are
embedding AI agents directly into their core products. This strengthens the importance of owning the
runtime layer that orchestrates live conversations.


**6. Champion governance and trust.** Mission-critical conversations require reliability, auditability and
privacy by design. Embed compliance, data sovereignty and policy control into the core platform and
your values. Governance can be a source of differentiation, not overhead. vCons (a new data standard)
are a potential asset for Intelligent Engagement players in this respect.


**7. Remain AI-Model neutral.** Enterprises need flexibility in a fast-moving model market. Platforms
that allow models to be swapped without architectural disruption become the stabilising layer in
enterprise AI Voice deployments.


**8. Address Sovereignty requirements.** In some markets, broadly most outside of the US and China,
national-level data sovereignty is an increasingly pressing concern. If these are the markets you serve,
ensure that your proposition clearly addresses these needs. Even within the US, sovereignty is an
issue at a company level. Customers want to be in control of their options, and be able to implement
sovereignty if and when required.


**9. Leverage network advantage where relevant.** For CSPs, integrating AI Voice with Network APIs
and network-native capabilities can create scalable, high-quality services that are difficult for pure
software providers to replicate.

**10. Deepen Enterprise Integration.** Embed AI Voice into CRM, workforce management, identity and
