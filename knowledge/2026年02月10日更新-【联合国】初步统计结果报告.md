# **NOTE ON METHODOLOGY**

This preliminary report examines developments in global major tropical fruits trade in 2025. The analysis is based on
provisional full-year estimates that were compiled and constructed from the following sources: country responses to the
2025 questionnaire of the FAO Subgroup on Tropical Fruits; data from the UN Comtrade database and Trade Data Monitor
Inc.; and secondary data and information from desk research. The findings incorporate preliminary monthly trade data as
well as information from industry sources as available up to the beginning of November 2025. Due to the customary lag
in the reporting of monthly trade data of approximately 40 days, full-year estimates in this report were generated from
monthly export data by country as reported up to July/August 2025, and on monthly import data as reported up to
August/September 2025. FAO is continuously monitoring global trade flows of major tropical fruits and will update this
report in the second quarter of 2026 when official data for 2025 have been released and validated.


All data refer to global trade of fresh or dried major tropical fruits, as covered by HS codes 080430 (pineapple); 080440
(avocado); 080450 (mango, mangosteen and guava); and 080720 (papaya) under the harmonized tariff nomenclature
system of the World Customs Organization. Data on the import volumes of the European Union exclude the United
Kingdom of Great Britain and Northern Ireland since February 2020.


Unless otherwise specified, the source of the figures is the author(s)' own elaboration from various data sources as
explained above.


All data in this report should be considered as provisional.

Preliminary data indicate that the volume of world trade

risen to a new peak of over USD 12 billion in constant
2014–2016-dollar terms, marking an increase of
approximately 9 percent from 2024 (Figure 1). This would
indicate a continuation of the rapid and
near-uninterrupted expansion seen over the previous
decade, largely on account of ample supplies and
demand-driven growth.


As in most years, weather conditions and their respective
effects on supplies were mixed across regions and
commodities. Favorable weather conditions resulted in
greater production quantities and higher fruit quality in
several major tropical fruit production areas, most
notably for mangoes in Brazil and Peru, and avocados in
Mexico and Peru. Meanwhile, large amounts of rainfall
and tropical storms caused production declines and
compromised fruit quality for supplies of pineapples



from Costa Rica, with exports from Costa Rica making up
57 percent of the global total over the 2021 to 2023
period, and of mangoes from Mexico and India.
Investments in production expansion furthermore drove
higher exports from several emerging suppliers, in
particular for avocados from Chile and Morocco.

diverging trends in exports from the different regions are
evident. While exports of major tropical fruits increased
from all regions, global market share rose most in South
America, followed by Africa. On the other hand, exports
from Central America and the Caribbean grew at a slower
pace, resulting in a reduced share of global exports.


On the import side, firm demand was reported in both
the United States of America and the European Union,
the two main importers, particularly for avocados,
mangoes and pineapples. However, against a generally
strong supply situation for avocados and mangoes,

# **Commodity Briefs** **Mango, mangosteen and** **guava**

## **_Exports_**


Global exports of mango, mangosteen and guava [3] are
estimated to have grown to approximately 2.7 million
tonnes in 2025, an increase of 7 percent from the
previous year. Higher exports of mangosteen from
Thailand, as well as of mangoes from Brazil, Egypt and
Peru would be the main driving factor behind this. In
terms of export quantities by type at the global level,
mango is estimated to have accounted for around
85 percent of global shipments and mangosteen for
around 15 percent. As previously, guava has continued to
display a low availability in import markets, in particular
due to its lower suitability for transport.


Available monthly trade data up to August, and industry
information up to November 2025, suggest that exports
from Mexico, the leading exporter of mangoes to world
markets, are likely to grow by an estimated 2 percent
over the full year, to some 450 000 tonnes. As this is a
lower rate of expansion than the overall estimated sector
growth of 7 percent, the country is thereby estimated to



see its market share in global mango exports drop to an
estimated 16 percent in 2025, from 17 to 20 percent in
previous years. Industry sources reported that an early
start of the mango season in Mexico had resulted in an
early end to the harvest, thereby disturbing regular
market activities. High amounts of rainfall additionally
impacted fruit quality negatively, leading to downward
price pressure. According to trade data by destination
provided by the Mexico National Institute of Statistics,
some 89 percent of Mexican mangoes were destined for
the United States of America, and some 9 percent for
Canada over the first eight months of 2025. In view of
lower qualities, the average export unit value of
shipments to this destination decreased by 12 percent
year-on-year over this period, to USD 1 187 per tonne.


Exports from Thailand, the leading supplier
of mangosteens to world markets, are estimated to have
risen by some 8 percent in 2025, to approximately
450 000 tonnes, as production in the country as well as
import demand from China remained favourable. On
average, China procures some 55 to 60 percent of Thai
exports of mangosteen, typically at high prices as the
premium quality of Thai fruits is habitually met with firm
consumer demand in China. In 2025, shipments from

increase, as the improved logistics facilitated the
shipment of higher quality fruits, which were able to

## **_Imports_**


Total global import quantities of fresh mangoes,
mangosteens, and guavas are estimated to have risen by
12 percent to 2.6 million tonnes in 2025, as suggested by
available monthly trade data up to September/October.
The United States of America and the European Union
will remain the two leading global importers, with
estimated import shares of 23 percent and 17 percent,
respectively. In both markets, consumer demand for
mangoes reportedly remained solid, driven by a
mounting nutritional awareness of the assumed health
benefits of these fruits. Besides demand-side factors,
imports into the United States of America typically
depend on developments in supplies from Mexico, the
main origin of mangoes in the United States of America.
However, in 2025, strong growth in procurements from
Peru was additionally reported, facilitated by higher
export supplies from this origin and a 45-percent drop in
average unit values. Overall, imports into the United

7 percent in 2025, to approximately 600 000 tonnes.

substantially amid ample availability of quality export
quantities from Peru and Brazil. Imports into the

average unit values at the import stage over the first nine
months of the year.

# **Pineapple**

## **_Exports_**


Based on preliminary trade data, global exports of
pineapples are estimated to have shrunk by
approximately 5 percent in 2025, to 3.4 million tonnes,
determined largely by significant supply shortages in
Costa Rica, the world’s leading exporter with a market
share of around 60 percent. According to industry
information, weather conditions in key Costa Rican
growing areas were characterised by excessive rainfall
and high temperatures, which caused delays in planting,
stress on plants and higher incidences of pest
infestations. Shipments from Costa Rica are accordingly
estimated to have dropped by some 16 percent in 2025,
to about 2 million tonnes. In terms of leading
destinations, pineapple shipments from Costa Rica
continued to be almost exclusively destined to the
United States of America and the European Union, where

very moderate year-on-year increase of 1 percent.

constrained by strong domestic demand, avocado
shipments from Peru expanded substantially on the back
of production expansion and rising import demand.
Together, these two exporters supply over 60 percent of
total traded quantities to world markets, with shipments
primarily destined for the United States of America and
the European Union. Higher exports were also reported

# **Papaya**

favourable weather conditions had been supporting
steady supplies of quality papayas throughout most of

improved export opportunities. Virtually all Mexican
papaya exports are destined for the United States of
