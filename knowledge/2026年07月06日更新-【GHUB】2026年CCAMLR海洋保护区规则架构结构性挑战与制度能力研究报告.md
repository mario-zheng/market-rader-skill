### Executive Summary

Despite the establishment of two Marine Protected

Areas (MPAs), fundamental issues stemming from the

definition of an MPA continue to surface in negotiations

within the Commission for the Conservation of Antarctic

Marine Living Resources (CCAMLR). As a pioneer in

the establishment of protected areas in areas beyond

national jurisdiction (ABNJ), CCAMLR designated the

South Orkney Islands Southern Shelf MPA and the

Ross Sea Region MPA in 2009 and 2016, respectively.

However, several institutional issues continue to

plague CCAMLR's MPA negotiations. Over the past

decade, although new MPA proposals have emerged

continuously, no new consensus has been reached on

their establishment. Instead, fundamental questions,

such as the definition of an MPA, are frequently revisited

in discussion. Even though the newly effective United

Nations Agreement on Marine Biodiversity in Areas

Beyond National Jurisdiction (the BBNJ Agreement)

includes a definition of MPA that reflects an updated

global consensus, discussions regarding the definition

within CCAMLR persist. Based on international

environmental regime theory and integrated with

the research framework of international institutional

effectiveness, we provide a comprehensive exposition

of CCAMLR's institutional attributes and dilemmas by

analyzing its institutional background, operational

mechanisms, and the structural challenges it faces.


MPAs in CCAMLR should not be understood as isolated

conservation concepts, but rather as comprehensive

policy tools embedded within the broader institutional

frameworks of the Antarctic Treaty System, the United

Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea (UNCLOS),

and the fisheries governance regime under the Food

and Agriculture Organization (FAO). In legal and

institutional terms, CCAMLR MPAs are conservation

measures adopted pursuant to Article IX of the CAMLR

Convention, deriving legitimacy from CCAMLR’s broad

ecosystem conservation mandate rather than from a



narrow fisheries management function alone. As an

international regime combining fisheries management

and ecosystem conservation, CCAMLR has developed

a science-based decision-making structure centered

on the Scientific Committee (SC-CAMLR) and guided

by the principle of “best available science.” Working

groups, monitoring-control-surveillance (MCS)

mechanisms, and compliance evaluation procedures

provide institutional support for the planning and

implementation of MPAs. At the same time, CCAMLR

operates under a consensus-based decision-making

system in which every member effectively holds veto

power. While this enhances institutional legitimacy, it

also significantly increases the difficulty of reaching

an agreement.


There are several defining characteristics of CCAMLR

MPAs. First, they are typically remote, large-scale or

even ultra-large MPAs, making planning, monitoring,

and enforcement extremely costly. Second, MPAs

themselves constitute conservation measures within

CCAMLR’s broader regulatory framework and

therefore must pass through both scientific review and

political negotiation processes. Third, many proposed

MPAs geographically overlap with areas linked to

Antarctic territorial claims, creating unavoidable

geopolitical sensitivities.


By tracing the historical evolution of CCAMLR’s

MPA discussions, we show that although CCAMLR

established a general framework for MPAs and

successfully adopted the South Orkney Islands

Southern Shelf MPA in 2009 and the Ross Sea Region

MPA in 2016, negotiations over proposed MPAs in East

Antarctica, the Weddell Sea, and the Western Antarctic

Peninsula have remained unresolved for years. Since

2012, MPAs have become one of the most politically

contentious issues within CCAMLR.



II


Applying the institutional effectiveness framework, we

argue that the core challenge facing CCAMLR MPAs

lies in the tension between “problem structure” and

“institutional capacity.” Compared with traditional

fisheries management, MPAs represent a more

politically “malign” issue because they involve deeper

conflicts between individual and collective interests.

Some members continue to view CCAMLR primarily

as a fisheries management regime and therefore

oppose spatial restrictions on fishing activities,

while supporters of MPAs emphasize ecosystem

conservation and global public goods. These disputes

are rooted not only in material interests but also in

competing values and long-term visions, making

compromise particularly difficult. In addition, the

Antarctic marine ecosystem remains scientifically

uncertain, while climate change further complicates

predictions regarding conservation outcomes and

the distribution of costs and benefits. Although

CCAMLR has substantially strengthened its scientific

capacity, institutional arrangements, monitoring

systems, and stakeholder engagement over the



past two decades, these improvements have not been

sufficient to overcome persistent political divisions

among members.


We conclude that the experience of CCAMLR

demonstrates that establishing MPAs in ABNJ is not

merely a scientific or technical exercise, but also a

profoundly political process shaped by competing

interests, values, and geopolitical dynamics. As the

Agreement on the Conservation and Sustainable

Use of Marine Biological Diversity of Areas Beyond

National Jurisdiction (BBNJ Agreement) moves toward

implementation, new efforts to establish MPAs on

the high seas will accelerate globally. The lessons and

challenges emerging from CCAMLR therefore provide

valuable insights for future global ocean governance:

scientific consensus alone is insufficient to secure

effective conservation outcomes; institutional

design, political trust, and sustained commitment

to multilateral cooperation are equally essential to

institutional effectiveness.

表明，仅有约一半的已划定的海洋保护区得到实际执法，

而处于强保护或完全保护状态的海洋保护区甚至不足一

半（Sala et al., 2018）。由于同一海域存在多方利益竞

争，划定禁捕区或高强度保护区往往面临困难（Li and

Fluharty, 2017）。与此同时，公共资源不足与治理能力

有限导致的执法缺失，也是许多海洋保护区普遍面临的

挑战（Rife et al., 2013）。

关于海洋保护区在海洋养护方面的作用，学界一直存

在争议。一些观点认为，海洋保护区（尤其是禁捕

型海洋保护区）是解决资源过度开发问题的关键途径

（Giakoumi et al., 2018）；而另一些观点则将禁捕型

海洋保护区仅视为应对过度捕捞的众多管理措施之一。

Caveen 等人指出，尽管有充分的证据表明海洋保护区

在养护生物多样性方面有效，但其有益于渔业的证据却

相对薄弱（Caveen et al., 2015）。Caveen 进一步声称，

如果以养护为导向的渔业管理能够得到充分执行，将比

依赖海洋保护区更有效地解决过度捕捞问题（Caveen

et al., 2014）。也有观点认为，将争论聚焦于海洋保

护区对渔业的影响具有误导性，因为这实质上将海洋



保护区视为渔业管理工具，而非生物多样性养护工具。

当养护对象是整个生态系统或栖息地时，海洋保护区的

目标在于维护生物多样性所提供的多重生态系统服务，

并最大化其对人类福祉的贡献（Lester and Halpern,

2008）。事实上，渔业资源只是划定海洋保护区时需要

考虑的诸多生态系统服务之一。回顾渔业管理屡遭失败

的历史，Roberts 在其著作《假如海洋空荡荡》的最后一

章中提出一种新的海洋保护区范式：禁捕型海洋保护区

应成为海洋生物多样性管理的基础性支柱，而非仅限于

少数区域的特殊安排（Roberts, 2007）。在可用资源有

限且现有开发利益相互竞争的前提下，海洋保护区最终

可能只能覆盖少数关键区域。然而，这种范式转变有助

于决策者以更具战略性和系统性的方式选择这些区域。



在此有必要澄清几种关于海洋保护区的常见误解。第一，

并非所有海洋保护区都是禁捕区。IUCN 的保护地分类

明确指出，只要符合海洋保护区的养护目标，某些类型

海洋保护区可以允许捕捞活动。《BBNJ 协定》中对海

洋保护区的定义也持相同理解。如前文所述，海洋保护

区是由其养护目标而非管理方式来界定的。第二，海洋

保护区并不意味着永久关闭某一海域。诚然，实现养护

海洋保护区是一个在不同制度场景下被赋予不同内涵的

一般性概念，因此需要放在具体的制度场景中进行理解。

从资源养护的角度出发，决定行动的关键不是对一个一

般性的概念是否存在共同理解，而是是否针对特定区域

的具体养护目标达成共识，进而通过具体措施实现这些

目标。在这一点上，海洋保护区和其他实质性的渔业捕

捞规则并无本质区别。因此，关于保护区的决策，无论

是国内层面还是国际层面，只有聚焦于具体的养护目标，

才有可能向前推进。



然而，从保护区建设的实际成效来看，制度能力与挑战之

间的差距仍未弥合。近年来，CCAMLR 将西南极半岛保护

区与磷虾渔业管理升级开展协同的努力，体现了其对海洋

保护区建设的承诺和进一步开展制度创新的意愿。然而，

新的国际政治大环境也在影响CCAMLR 集体决策所需的合

作意愿。这就需要更多有能力的国家发挥影响力，维护多

边主义和国际合作。



随着《BBNJ 协定》的生效，其他国家管辖范围以外海域

的保护区建设也将正式启动。理解CCAMLR 在海洋保护

区方面所面临挑战的问题结构和制度设置，有助于理解

其他海洋保护区建设中可能出现的相似问题。同时，在

从CCAMLR 海洋保护区共识建设过程中汲取经验和教训

时，也需要注意CCAMLR 独有的制度和物理背景。



现实中，利益、价值观、偏好差异与不确定性相互交织，

共同决定了南极海洋保护区在政治层面是一项更具有挑战

性的决策。CCAMLR 作为在南大洋被赋予关键养护授权的

国际制度，已经针对这些挑战持续开展了制度能力建设。
