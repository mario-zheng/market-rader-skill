**KEY TAKEAWAYS FROM BERNSTEIN FUTURE OF TECH CONFERENCE**

Bernstein hosted its Future of Tech conference in Singapore on 14th May 2025 with a panel discussion on ‘Gen AI in asset
management’ with Mark Lam (Director, Index Fixed Income at BlackRock), Mark Chua (Head of Investment Science at Maybank
Asset Management) and Eugene Ong (Head of APAC Sales Financial Service at AlphaSense). The panel discussion covered
topics ranging from how organizations should think about structuring their AI programs ie. top-down vs. bottoms-up approach,
building internally or buying readily available solutions to discussing some practical use-cases of Gen AI for investment teams
and addressing the dreaded question of ‘Does Gen AI take our jobs?’. We share below some key takeaways:


**Top-down vs. bottom-up approach:** When thinking about structuring AI programs within organizations, there are two
approaches that can be (has been) adopted- the top-down approach where AI programs have more organizational buyin, is more structured and results in wider roll-out of AI tools across departments. However, it tends to be a bit slower in
implementation and runs the risk of tools not being used by investment teams. The other approach is more bottoms-up where
use cases come from investment teams and then AI solutions are build to solve for those use-cases- this is an approach that
BlackRock adopts while Maybank highlighted that their approach is a blend of the two. In our conversation with multiple funds
over the past few months, we noted that both these approaches are being adopted depending on the team structures and
management preference.


**Build vs. buy:** Given the availability of resources and technical expertise, BlackRock is building most AI solutions internally while
also partnering with Microsoft. Maybank is also building tools internally given the quant team is leading the initiative. However,
in most of our conversation with PMs/CIOs in the last few months, we found that most funds, especially long-only funds, are
preferring to find a readily available solution rather than building capabilities internally. Only where funds have quant teams
who can lead the initiative, are trying to build internal solutions and there are some funds who have found it useful to build for
certain use-cases while go for vendors for other use-cases. AlphaSense, is clearly on the buy side of this equation and Eugene
highlighted that one of the key reasons why their clients like to use AlphaSense or any other vendor like them is because of the
money that is spent at these platforms on R&D to keep up with the pace of development on Gen AI front which gets difficult for
large organization's internal AI programs. Over and above that, the vendor solutions have already spent time and money to train
the LLMs on external financial market data and are offering capabilities to upload internal datasets; making it futile for asset
managers to do the same internally.


**Clean/systematic internal data-capture is paramount:** All our panelists couldn’t stress more about the importance of a
strong data fabric internally to make an AI program successful and scalable. The internal data capture would cover emails, BBG
charts, Slack messages, internal investment memos, company meeting notes etc. All the information which could help get more
investment insights should be captured in a more systematic manner, even if that has not been done historically. In our last
year’s fireside chat with GIC, the same point was made to enable more scalable and structured adoption of AI. For details, see Quant Series: Leveraging Gen AI for Asset Management -Key takeaways from our chat with GIC


**Impact on jobs/hiring:** One of the biggest worry (understandably so) for investment analysts and PMs has been about
the impact that Gen AI would have on their jobs. All our panelists agreed that Gen AI should be thought as augmenting the
investment practitioners rather than replacing them. Eugene shared what one of the PMs at a big asset manager highlighted
to him- while his team was becoming 20% more efficient with the use of AlphaSense, the focus has been to use that additional
time to do more productive work rather than reduce the headcount. In our conversations, while many PMs have argued that
these Gen AI tools can act as an intern and are expected to reach the level of a junior analyst; most of them still believe that
removing humans from the equation is not a viable/trustworthy option. We believe that Gen AI would continue to redefine the
scope of work for the junior analysts as they might end up spending much less time on information or data gathering and more
time on deriving investment insights and/or idea generation. However, we would argue that more AI adoption would mean less
hiring going forward as the same number of team members would potentially be able to cover more sectors and stocks in a
more efficient way. To some extent it is paradoxical, as on one side, AI is being termed as the big solution to solve the ageing
demographic challenge while on the other side, it is limiting job opportunities for people who are willing/able to work.

At BlackRock, multiple investment team members are working on AI initiatives with a core focus on setting up the right
infrastructure to make AI solutions scalable. The equities team is using Gen AI for deep research, creating agents to mimic

**USE CASES SHARED BY THE PANELISTS**

Mark Chua, Head of Investment Science at Maybank Asset Management shared couple of examples of how Gen AI is being used
in their investing team

- **[Using AI to bridge the gap between quants and fundamentals and make quant outputs easy to understand:]** [ He]
shared that they have created a Dashboard which gives a quick summary of any company with information about its factor/
quant scores, technicals and fundamentals. They use AI to generate a summary which explains why the quant framework
likes a particular company, making it more user-friendly for the fundamental team and removing the need for a quant PM to
explain the model output. Exhibit 1 below shows a screenshot of the dashboard.


- **[Using prompt engineering to analyze earnings calls:]** [ He shared that they use prompt engineering to extract the right]
kind of financial metrics once an earnings call is uploaded into ChatGPT. This is based on the kind of information that a
fundamental analyst in their team would typically look for in an earnings call. Given the engineered GPT is able to generate
output in seconds, it has reduced the time spent by their fundamental team to analyze earnings call. More interestingly, the
prompt engineering has helped them get an earnings forecast for next period along with confidence level of prediction
(roughly in the range of 80%) from the AI tool which has been quite useful. Exhibit 2 below shows a screenshot of the output
of this prompt engineered GPT. This is modeled based on a Chicago Booth paper released last year, which claimed that LLMs
are able to analyze financial statements better than an average analyst (do note the working paper has been withdrawn as
some inconsistencies were found).


- **[Using Gen AI to respond to RFPs:]** [ Responding to RFPs (request for proposal) takes away investment team’s time, which]
could be used more productively for investing. MayBank is using Gen AI to quickly respond to RFPs by using historical
repository of questions and usual responses. Some other funds have also highlighted this as a low hanging use-case to
increase productivity of the investment teams.


Mark Lam, Director, Index Fixed Income at BlackRock highlighted that they have taken a pull-approach when generating AI
solutions for investment teams ie. the use-cases come from investment teams rather than the AI team generating general
solutions. Mark shared two use-cases where Gen AI has been able to reduce a lot of time and effort

- **[To analyze and understand the bond prospectuses:]** [ Given bond prospectuses are bulky documents, the team has been]
able to save a lot of time by using LLMs to analyze, summarize and understand these documents. This allows the team to
cover more securities with greater speed and consistency.


- **[Augmenting the investment process:]** [ One example that Mark shared was with respect to the primary market process]
on ETFs, which is a lengthy process, requiring negotiations, and back and forth between multiple teams. With AI that
process has been shortened as historical responses can be used to expedite negotiations rather than going back to multiple
stakeholders every time.

**MORE USE CASES FOR INVESTING- BASED ON CLIENT CONVERSATIONS**

One of the most pertinent question that was asked on the back of our last report on Future of Tech : Gen AI in Asset
Management was about how other investors are using Gen AI solutions in investment workflows. In our last report, we had
shown examples of how other funds have been using Gen AI across different areas of investing such as research, portfolio
optimization, risk assessment & management, client personalization, sentiment analysis, scenario analysis etc. which we show
again in Exhibit 5 below.


Last year GIC’s Portfolio Manager, Eu Jin Teoh joined us for a fireside chat to discuss Gen AI in asset management and shared
some use cases. As a quant, he found, the most intuitive use case for LLMs as being a coding intern which makes it easier,
especially for non-quants to generate a piece of code very quickly (and in most instance requiring minimal editing). Many
quant funds that we have spoken to have achieved 20% time save on an average by using Gen AI for coding purposes. From
a fundamental analyst standpoint, Eu Jin had highlighted some other use cases such as summarization and synthesis of many
documents on cross-sectional basis; for example, comparing products, strategy and business models across companies in an
industry using 10K. Using LLMs to perform time series trends; for example, creating a summary of how a business has evolved
and how the focus of management has changed over a period of time using their annual reports. Using Gen AI to translate
images into text; for example, it can be used to get deep insights with reasoning from a cash flow statement image of the
company. Exhibit 6 below shows screen-shot of the cross-sectional and time series analysis using company filings. For details
on all other examples, see- Quant Series: Leveraging Gen AI for Asset Management -Key takeaways from our chat with GIC


We now add more use cases for investing (we expect this list to keep growing) which are based on our conversations with
various funds over the past couple of months in Exhibit 7 below. These conversations have been with asset managers and
owners, long-only and HFs, quant and fundamental teams across Asia, Europe and US. We present below some insights 

- **[Investment teams at early stages of adoption. Quant/HFs leading]** [: Overall, we found that Gen AI adoption is still in]
early stages, with some quant/HFs being more advanced in their AI journey. There are certain regions where AI is still not
being explored at an enterprise level while the implementation/usage is high in US, London, Singapore and HK. Some CIOs
highlighted that they are just starting to think about how Gen AI should be used by investing teams.


- **[Both centralized and de-centralized approaches for AI initiatives:]** [ In some funds, the AI programs are being centrally]
driven where some tools are being rolled-out at an enterprise level while at other funds, AI initiatives are being driven
by someone from the investment team. Funds with quant capabilities have asked their quant teams to take lead in
implementation or creating solutions for the investment teams and in these instances, more use-cases are already in
production.


- **[Focus is on using Gen AI to improve productivity:]** [ Most funds are still focussed on how Gen AI solutions can improve]
productivity for the investment team. In Exhibit 7 below, we have highlighted use-cases of how funds are trying to bring more
efficiency in their research process or information gathering or RFP responses or client/regulatory reporting etc. Do note,
some quant teams are working on building better predictive models using Gen AI but for the broader industry, the aim still
is to use Gen AI to help investment teams become better at what they do, and use the saved time to do more interesting
research or help in idea generation.


- **[Lot of effort in building robust internal datasets/dashboards:]** [ Not many funds have been historically keeping a]
systematic record of meeting notes (internal or external), or sector-specific data/resources etc. in a central database. Many
funds are now working towards building a more cohesive database which can interact with AI tools to extract interesting
and meaningful insights. There is a growing realization that the decades of knowledge which is sitting in someone’s
notebook or desktop, if brought together, can become extremely powerful. Some funds have taken another step of creating
internal knowledge base to share ‘the right prompt to get to the right output’ and designing training programs for their
investment teams. We would argue that all funds should think about a systematic and regular knowledge sharing session
where investment teams come together say every month and share the most interesting use-case they found or the most
interesting vendor solution they came across.


- **[Most funds looking for readily available vendor solutions:]** [ Baring big funds with reasonable internal technical know how]
or quant teams, most funds are interested in finding readily available AI solutions. Some of the vendors which are being used/
explored by funds are AlphaSense, Fintool, Boosted.ai, NotebookLM, otter.ai, Ravenpack, Bloomberg/FactSet AI and Copilot
to name a few. Cost per seat and how much the tool has been trained on their own market remains a key consideration when
