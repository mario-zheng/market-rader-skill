## **VC activity**

VC activity surged in the second quarter of 2024, fueled by three billion-dollar rounds. We tracked

$30.8 billion in Q2 VC funding for AI & ML companies, including xAI’s $6.0 billion round. xAI’s

Series B megadeal includes solely financial VC investors, which is unique among other leading

foundation model startups. Financial VC Thrive Capital is also stepping in to support OpenAI.

Aside from xAI’s outlier, other top deals continued to be dominated by tech giant investment.

NVIDIA participated in four of the top five deals this quarter: CoreWeave’s $1.1 billion round,

Mistral AI’s Series B, Scale AI’s Series F with Amazon and Meta, and Wayve’s billion-dollar round

alongside Microsoft. Despite corporate VC investment, deal count remains significantly lower than

predownturn counts, staying consistent over the past seven quarters. However, for startups that

can raise, valuations continue to rise, with median pre-money valuations for both early- and late
stage deals reaching new highs of $28.2 million and $50.0 million, respectively.


In Q2, VC exit activity rebounded after previous down quarters, with both exit value and count

rising. Buoyed by Tempus AI’s $5.7 billion public listing at a 10.9x EV/revenue multiple, exit value

rose to $11.5 billion, its highest level since Q2 2022. (NVIDIA’s ongoing acquisition of Run:ai,

announced earlier in Q2 but delayed by regulatory review, is not included in this total.) This was

the first of two major acquisitions, with the company also acquiring Deci, a platform that optimizes

AI models for hardware efficiency. These deals, in total an estimated $1.3 billion, underscore

the investment hardware providers are willing to make to bolster their software ecosystems.

Competitor AMD has since followed these acquisitions with the acquisitions of Fireworks AI and

Silo AI for a combined $717.0 million, showing the concentration of M&A among acquirers with the

highest-performing stocks. Other outlier acquisitions included Hyundai’s $923.0 million purchase

of Motional and JFrog’s $230.0 million acquisition of Qwak. We do not track the hiring of founding

teams at Adept and Character AI by Amazon and Google, respectively, as acquisitions, although

they show the challenges of scaling valuations for foundation model companies.

**SELECT COMPANY HIGHLIGHTS: ANTHROPIC**


**Overview**


Anthropic further solidified its position as a prominent foundational model provider with the

launch of its latest model from the Claude series in June. Claude 3.5 Sonnet is the first release in

the Claude 3.5 model family, with two more releases expected to come later this year. The new

model is faster and cheaper than Claude 3 Opus released in March, placing its pricing more in

line with competitors Gemini 1.5 Pro and Chat-GPT 4o. Anthropic reports that the new model

outperforms these competitors across benchmarking metrics like graduate-level reasoning, code

generation, and multilingual math. [17] The LiveBench LLM evaluation suite scores Claude 3.5 Sonnet

the highest across all metrics, including a significant difference in coding skill over GPT-4o. [18]


To better respond to these complex coding projects, the model has a new user interface (UI)

feature called Artifacts. Artifacts creates a separate window where both Claude and the user can

edit and run code, establishing a dedicated workspace to build on the model’s outputs unseen in

other leading models. In addition to the upgraded performance and UI, the new model includes a

prompt generation and testing feature to better tailor model outputs to business needs.

**SELECT COMPANY HIGHLIGHTS: ANTHROPIC**


Q2 also saw a large secondary transaction of Anthropic shares through the liquidation of FTX’s

stake in the company. Through secondary markets, FTX was able to liquidate two-thirds of its

original $500.0 million stake in Anthropic. [19] Bids for the shares valued the company at $23.0 billion,

an increase from the previous $19.4 billion valuation. [20]


**Leadership**


In Q2, several former OpenAI employees left for Anthropic. In May, former co-lead of OpenAI’s

Superalignment team Jan Leike joined Anthropic. Leike previously resigned from OpenAI citing

disagreements with leadership and concerns over safety culture in a thread on X. [21] John Schulman,

a co-founder of OpenAI and co-lead of the post-training team, also left OpenAI to join Anthropic’s

alignment team. The moves underscore Anthropic’s investment in developing safe models.


**Competitive landscape**


Through its partnership with AWS, Anthropic has carved out a large enterprise customer base

in the highly competitive foundation model space. The release of Claude 3.5 Sonnet and its

accompanying suite of features tailor its offerings further to this customer base. Its improved

ability to handle complex instructions allows the model to better serve customer support or

multistep workflow needs. While OpenAI remains the most widely adopted enterprise model,

usage of two models is common. [22] This currently positions Anthropic as a complement to OpenAI,

particularly as AWS does not support OpenAI.


**Outlook**


With high quality and lower costs, Claude 3.5 Sonnet represents a legitimate alternative to OpenAI.

Additionally, the focus on safety and corporate partnerships will continue to appeal to enterprise

customers. However, with OpenAI expected to set a new bar for performance with the release of

GPT-5, Anthropic will have to build upon the successes of its new model. To keep pace, Anthropic

is expected to release two new models later this year, with Claude 3.5 Opus and Haiku, and also

improve its enterprise product offerings.
