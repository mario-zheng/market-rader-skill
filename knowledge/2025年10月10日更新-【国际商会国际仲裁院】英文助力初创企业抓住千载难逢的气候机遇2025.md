## **Aryam**
#### Biodiversity & Nature-Based | Paris, France | Website | Est. 2023

Where AI meets nature to build climate-resilient agriculture

### Team

Aryam is led by CEO and founder Rim Tehraoui, former BNP Paribas Group Chief Data & AI Officer and
ESG risk management lead, former Chair of OS Climate-Linux Foundation and current chair of Hub
France AI.

### Business case

Industrial farming has driven soil degradation, biodiversity loss, and rising greenhouse gas emissions—
undermining yields and revenues. Today, agrifood actors face mounting environmental, regulatory,
and economic pressures to shift toward climate-resilient, biodiversity-friendly practices. Yet widespread
adoption remains stalled by technical complexity, perceived risks, and a lack of tailored financing.

### Value proposition

Aryam accelerates the agroecological transition by delivering AI-powered, hyper-contextualized
recommendations, universal access to agroecological knowledge, and robust measurement of soil
health, carbon, biodiversity, and productivity—making the transition effective, scalable, and fundable.
Its integrated platform empowers corporations and institutions to design, implement, and track largescale programs, while its AI assistant equips farmers and agri-advisors with localised, actionable
guidance to boost resilience, productivity, and impact reporting.

### Business model

Aryam works mainly with institutional actors underwriting large-scale transition projects that strengthen
smallholders’ climate-resilience and de-risk supply chains. It also provides its Gen-AI agroecology
assistant directly to farmers and agri-advisors, bringing cutting-edge support to the field. Partnerships
with organisations like JuST Institute, Entrepreneurs du Monde and INRA help expand its reach.

### Impact

Aryam supports a large-scale agroforestry project involving 100,000 farmers, 500,000 hectares, and
more than 50 million trees by 2030. Through its partnership with the JuST Institute, Aryam is also
reaching out to 52 microfinance institutions across 32 countries to spread regenerative practices.

## **Carbix Corp**
#### Clean Energy Transition | Quincy, MA | Website | Est. In 2020

Carbix mineral carbonation reactors transform CO2 emissions into raw cement and concrete materials
that decrease building materials’ embodied carbon.

### Team

Carbix is led by Co-Founders Johann Q. Sammy (CEO), a mechanical engineer, Vinit V. Dighe (Science
Officer), a PhD in Aerodynamics, and Nicole Howard (Lead Chemical & Materials Engineer). They
previously started a small wind turbine company.

### Business case

Cement production accounts for 8% of global CO2 emissions, with one ton releasing about a ton of
CO2. Carbix helps cement makers cut emissions and turn captured CO2 into valuable byproducts, while
lowering CO2 capture costs compared to traditional methods.

### Value proposition

Carbix offers a mineral carbonation reactor that captures CO2 on-site and turns it into raw materials for
cement. This system is affordable, scalable, and modular. It helps cement plants cut emissions and earn
revenue by selling the carbonate products it produces. The reactor speeds up CO2 mineralization using
advanced mixing, pressure control, UV-C light, and energy recovery.

### Business model

Carbix sells modular CO2 capturing reactors to industrial emitters, producing carbonates sold to
buyers like cement wholesalers and chemical manufacturers. Revenue comes from equipment sales,
carbonate sales through long-term agreements, and revenue-sharing with some host sites.

### Impact

Carbix’s technology can cut up to 80% of CO2 emissions from cement production and replace
traditional cement with low-carbon alternatives. Each installation can capture over 100,000 metric tons
of CO2 per year.

## **CarbonTrac**
#### Sustainable Supply Chain & Manufacturing | London, UK | Website | Est. 2023

The first loyalty-integrated AI system that turns grocery shopping into scalable climate action

### Team

CarbonTrac is led by CEO and founder Yasmine Abdu, who has formed partnerships with the UN ITU,
UNESCO, Nestlé and Samsung. She is also an international speaker, having shared her vision on global
stages including SXSW and Vogue. She is supported by Dr. Yuriy Pankratov with 25+ years in data and
ML, Tony Milton with 20+ years in scalable SaaS solutions, and Julien Acone with 8+ years in design.

### Business case

34% of global emissions come from food systems, with over 65% linked to household spending. Yet
despite growing awareness, there’s a massive gap between consumer intent and action: 80% of Gen Z
and Millennials say they want to shop sustainably, but only 12% actually do. This intent-action gap is one
of the largest missed opportunities in climate action. CarbonTrac closes that gap by turning everyday
grocery decisions into simple, rewarding opportunities to cut emissions at scale.

### Value proposition

CarbonTrac is a SaaS platform that integrates with supermarket loyalty programs to track, rate, and
reward sustainable purchases. Its AI models calculate product footprints in minutes within 5-7% LCA
accuracy and suggest 2-3 greener swaps per item, paired with cashback rewards and emotional
prompts like “this saves a turtle.” CarbonTrac requires no extra steps, making sustainable choices
seamless, rewarding, and scalable across millions of baskets.

### Business model

CarbonTrac operates on a B2B SaaS and revenue-sharing model. Supermarkets pay subscription
fees for integration via API and share in the upside of 2-6% basket uplifts from sustainable shopping
behaviour. A portion of revenues funds cashback rewards for customers, while suppliers receive
financial support to transition to lower-carbon practices. This creates a profitable, circular model that
ensures carbon reduction aligns with commercial growth.

### Impact

CarbonTrac is on a mission to cut UK emissions by 5% by 2030 by shifting millions of everyday choices. The
initiative aims to influence supply chains, pushing retailers and suppliers toward lower-carbon practices.

## **Cellsense**
#### Circular Resource | New York City, NY | Website | Est. In 2022

Cellsense transforms algae and cellulose into compostable beads and fabrics that eliminate
microplastics across the design and cosmetic industries.

### Team

Aradhita Parasrampuria, Founder & CEO, holds an MFA in Textiles from Parsons and has worked at
Tory Burch, threeASFOUR, and Slow Factory Labs. She combines material science, design, and brand
partnership expertise to lead Cellsense’s R&D and collaborations.

### Business case

The $1.7 trillion fashion industry relies on plastic beads and sequins in one in five garments, contributing
to microplastic and heavy metal pollution. Regulatory pressures and consumer demand for
sustainability are growing, yet viable scalable alternatives to petroleum embellishments are scarce.
Cellsense delivers compostable, high-performance embellishments and fabrics for apparel, jewelry, and
skincare exfoliants.

### Value proposition

Cellsense transforms algae and cellulose into sustainable beads and beaded fabrics for the fashion,
jewelry, and beauty industries. Its high-performance material replaces microplastics while offering
designers a wide range of brilliant colors, clarities, and unique properties such as fluorescence. The
innovative manufacturing system deposits beaded designs directly onto fabric, shortening lead times
for custom products from weeks to days by eliminating expensive tooling and repetitive hand beading.

### Business model

Cellsense operates a B2B model, supplying compostable beads and automated beaded fabrics to
accessible, mid-range, and luxury brands across fashion, jewelry, and beauty. The company has
received 40+ inbound brand inquiries and completed four paid pilots (two in jewelry, one in fashion, and
one in beauty) with a 100% reorder rate, validating product-market fit. Future revenue includes pelletized
feedstock sales and technology licensing to scale sustainable solutions globally.

### Impact

By replacing just 1% of plastic embellishments, Cellsense could prevent 500 million beads daily from
entering oceans, cut 20 million metric tons of CO2 annually, reduce industrial water use by 430x
compared to synthetic dyes, and eliminate toxic heavy metals.

## **Ecoatex**

nonwovens, leather-alternative sheets).

### Team

Ecoatex is led by Founder and CEO Dr. Raha Saremi (PhD, MBA), whose background in polymer and fiber
sciences underpins the company’s platform for turning agricultural waste into high-performance, biodegradable
materials. She is a Phase I SBIR awardee (EPA & USDA) and an inventor on U.S. and international patent filings.

### Business case

The textile and leather industries are major polluters, consuming significant amounts of water and
energy and causing chemical pollution and greenhouse gas emissions. Synthetic alternatives are largely
fossil-based. Meanwhile, vast amounts of agricultural waste are burned or discarded, releasing CO2 and
wasting valuable materials. Ecoatex makes cellulose and lignin from agricultural residues; deforestationfree, local, and lower-carbon. Ecoatex valorizes existing agricultural byproducts, effectively turning waste
into value and helping customers meet sustainability targets at competitive cost.

### Value proposition

Ecoatex turns agricultural waste into sustainable, biodegradable fibers, yarns, nonwovens, and leather-alternative
sheet materials that use less water and energy, avoid PFAS and chrome, and reduce microplastic pollution. Built
from bio-based building blocks (cellulose, lignin, and nanocellulose) and advanced formats such as electrospun

enabling spec-driven, U.S.-made alternatives to conventional fossil-based materials at competitive cost.

### Business model

Ecoatex sells materials B2B and B2B2C to manufacturers in medical & hygiene, fashion, automotive, and
home goods. Pre-revenue today: The near-term plan is paid pilots/joint development agreements (JDAs),

selective licensing for wider market reach.

### Impact

Ecoatex cuts water use, chemical pollution, and CO2 emissions by repurposing waste, saving up to 2.5 kg CO2
per kg of material. They support farming communities and promote traceable, U.S.-sourced supply chains
while reducing microplastics and enabling safer end-of-life (including compostability where appropriate).

## **Savimbo Inc.**
#### Biodiversity & Nature-Based | Wilmington, DE | Website | Est. In 2022

Savimbo is a social enterprise made by, and for, Indigenous Peoples and local communities to access
climate markets directly.

### Team

Savimbo has three founders. Drea is an MD-technologist with 20+ years experience in high-tech, hard-science,
and serial entrepreneur. Jhony is a grassroots conservationist, Indigenous activist, and ex-WWF tracker with
20+ years protecting, studying, and advocating for the Tropical Andean biodiversity hotspot. Fernando is a
third-generation Colombian shaman with 20+ years in international Indigenous rights and conservation.

### Business case

Indigenous Peoples safeguard 30% of Earth’s intact ecosystems and 80% of its biodiversity, yet receive
less than 1% of climate funding. This gap limits their capacity to fight deforestation and manage
resources sustainably. Savimbo connects Indigenous communities directly to climate markets,
biodiversity, carbon, tree, and water credits, ecotourism, and agrobiodiversity crops, unlocking revenue,
improving livelihoods, and scaling conservation.

### Value proposition

Savimbo’s platform enables Indigenous communities to bypass intermediaries, sell six types of climate
products, and receive fair compensation. The social enterprise model includes a charitable arm that
advances land rights, literacy, and living conditions.

### Business model

B2B2C model serving enterprises, small businesses, and consumers looking to offset their climate
impact. Revenue comes from facilitating transactions for climate credits and sustainable products.

### Impact

Savimbo aims to support 1 billion Indigenous Peoples and local communities in 10 years to restore
the planet, transforming the way climate funding is distributed. By directly empowering Indigenous
communities, Savimbo not only addresses climate change but also fosters social equity and economic
resilience in traditionally underserved regions.
