## **Executive Summary**

This report marks the second phase of the project on health and wellness tourism in ASEAN
on “Resilience, Adaptability, and Competitiveness: A Roadmap for the Health and Wellness
Tourism Sector ASEAN SMEs in a Post-Crisis Age”. This report builds upon the insights from
the initial phase, which focused on Thailand, Viet Nam, Cambodia, and the Philippines.
This second phase expands its scope to include five additional countries: Malaysia,
Indonesia, Lao PDR, Singapore, and Brunei Darussalam. The primary objective remains
to enhance the competitiveness of ASEAN SMEs through cluster development
and adherence to international quality standards, fostering sustainable economic growth
in the post-crisis era.


ASEAN's health and wellness tourism sector has experienced remarkable growth,
establishing the region as a global medical and wellness tourism hub. Leading markets such
as Thailand, Malaysia, and Singapore continue to dominate, driven by competitive pricing,
exceptional service quality, and robust governmental support. Emerging players like
Cambodia and Lao PDR are steadily gaining traction, spurred by increasing investments
in healthcare infrastructure and distinctive wellness offerings.


However, the COVID-19 pandemic has profoundly reshaped the industry, presenting
opportunities and challenges. On the positive side, the pandemic has heightened
global health and wellness awareness, driving demand for preventive healthcare and
wellness-focused travel. Additionally, the accelerated adoption of digital health solutions,
including telemedicine, has enhanced accessibility and convenience for international
patients. Stringent hygiene and safety protocols introduced during the pandemic have also
bolstered global confidence in ASEAN’s healthcare systems. Furthermore, the crisis has
emphasised the importance of regional collaboration, fostering harmonised standards and
friendly competition.


Nevertheless, the industry faces significant hurdles in its recovery. Supply chain disruptions
have impacted the availability of medical equipment and wellness products. International
travel restrictions and evolving consumer preferences have reduced the number of medical
tourists, particularly from long-haul destinations. The cost of implementing enhanced health
and safety measures has increased operational expenses, disproportionately affecting
smaller businesses with limited resources. Moreover, intensified competition from other
regions adapting their health tourism strategies has added pressure on ASEAN countries
to remain competitive. These challenges necessitate strategic and coordinated efforts
to ensure recovery and long-term sustainability.


Health tourism clusters have emerged as a critical tool in enhancing competitiveness in this
complex environment. By fostering collaboration among SMEs, healthcare providers, and
government agencies, clusters enhance specialisation, raise service standards, and mitigate
market fragmentation and regional coordination issues.


Demand analysis highlights strong interest in health and wellness tourism across ASEAN
countries. Thailand, Malaysia, and Singapore benefit from well-established markets that
attract significant international tourist arrivals. Emerging markets such as Cambodia,
Indonesia, and Viet Nam exhibit high growth potential, driven by expanding middle-class
populations and increasing healthcare investments.


1


A high concentration of Joint Commission International-accredited hospitals reinforces
Thailand’s leadership. Singapore’s advanced healthcare technology and Malaysia’s globally
recognised halal healthcare offerings further bolster ASEAN’s competitive edge. Meanwhile,
emerging markets like Cambodia and Lao PDR leverage competitive labour costs and cultural
uniqueness to attract medical tourists.


The wellness tourism segment, which encompasses spas, holistic therapies, and cultural
wellness experiences, offers significant promise but remains underdeveloped in terms
of regional quality standards. Harmonisation efforts led by Malaysia and Indonesia are critical
to elevating service quality and ensuring competitiveness.


Key barriers, including limited access to financing, fragmented regional policies, and
persistent workforce shortages, continue to hinder SME growth in the sector. Governments
across ASEAN have responded with targeted measures, such as public-private partnerships
in Malaysia and capacity-building programmes in Viet Nam. These initiatives are crucial for
fostering a dynamic ecosystem that supports innovation and competitiveness.


Market segmentation reveals a diverse clientele, from medical tourists seeking cost-effective
procedures to wellness tourists prioritising relaxation and holistic well-being. Each ASEAN
country leverages its unique strengths: Thailand excels in cosmetic surgery, Malaysia
focuses on halal healthcare services, and Singapore specialises in complex medical
procedures. Emerging destinations like Cambodia integrate traditional Khmer medicine with
modern treatments, offering niche experiences that cater to specific market segments.


This report underscores the transformative potential of health and wellness tourism
as a driver of sustainable economic growth in ASEAN. However, the sector must adapt
to the evolving realities of a post-pandemic world. Strategic initiatives emphasising regional
collaboration, innovation, sustainability, and skills development are pivotal for long-term
success. By addressing challenges and seizing opportunities, ASEAN can solidify its position
as a global health and wellness tourism leader, delivering substantial economic and social
benefits for the region.

The ASEAN medical and wellness tourism market was valued at around US$66.1 billion
in 2023 and is estimated to reach approximately US$172.1 billion by the end of 2032,
at 12.7% compound annual growth rate (CAGR) over the forecast period.


One of the primary drivers is cost-effectiveness, with Thailand, Malaysia, and Indonesia
offering medical procedures at significantly lower prices than Western nations. For instance,
U.S. patients benefit from reduced treatment costs of 60%–80% in Malaysia, 25%–40%


3


in Singapore, and 50%–75% in Thailand, depending on the treatment. [1] This affordability
is complemented by high-quality healthcare infrastructure, especially in Thailand and
Singapore, where hospitals are accredited by international bodies like the JCI (Joint
Commission International). According to Thailand’s Medical Tourism Association, the country
attracts over 3 million international patients annually, particularly for cosmetic surgery, dental
care, and cardio treatments. [2]


Another major driver is government support across ASEAN countries, with governments
actively promoting medical and wellness tourism through policies, infrastructure investment,
and partnerships. Malaysia, for example, launched its Malaysia Healthcare Travel Industry
Blueprint 2021–2025, with the goal of establishing itself as the Cardiology Hub of Asia. [3]
Similarly, Singapore is recognised for its high-end, technologically advanced medical
services, especially in oncology and cardiology.


An influx of foreign direct investments in regional countries is also projected to play
a prominent role in the growth of the ASEAN medical and wellness tourism market.
For instance, the northern Quang Ninh province in Viet Nam recently became a hotspot for
foreign tourism investments. In 2023, the province welcomed an estimated 2.6 million foreign
visitors. Furthermore, in August 2024, the Viet Nam Tourism Property Association called for
foreign investment in real estate and other sectors across all 63 provinces of Viet Nam. [4]
The announcement attracted global investors, many of whom were interested in visiting
Viet Nam for further exploration. Similarly, in January 2024, the Council for the Development
of Cambodia announced an influx of US$4.92 billion as FDI for developing and expanding
various fields, including tourism in Cambodia. [5]


The growing demand for wellness tourism boosts the market as travellers seek holistic
treatments, rehabilitation, and traditional therapies alongside medical care. For example,
Indonesia and Thailand are known for offering wellness retreats that include spa treatments,
yoga, and alternative therapies, blending medical procedures with relaxation and recovery.


Lastly, the region’s geographic proximity to major markets like China, Australia, and
the Middle East contributes to the rising number of medical tourists. ASEAN's strategic
location and appeal as a travel destination make it a favourable option for medical and
wellness tourists, further driving market expansion.

The medical tourism market is expected to expand at a CAGR of 11.8%, reaching over
US$ 45.2 billion by 2032.


The medical tourism segment in the ASEAN region is one of the fastest-growing markets
globally. This is driven by a combination of factors. One key driver is affordability. Healthcare
costs in ASEAN countries are significantly lower than in Western nations, with treatments
in Thailand and Malaysia costing 50–70% less than in the U.S. or Europe. This cost
advantage attracts patients from developed countries seeking high-quality care at a fraction
of the price.


Quality of care is another critical factor. Thailand, Singapore, and Malaysia boast world-class
hospitals accredited by international bodies such as the JCI, ensuring high treatment
standards. Thailand’s Bumrungrad International Hospital, one of the leading medical tourism
hubs, serves over 500,000 international patients annually, reflecting the growing trust
in ASEAN healthcare services.


Active government involvement in consolidating healthcare infrastructure also plays a major
role in the growth of the ASEAN medical tourism market. For instance, in June 2023,
PT Pertamina Bina Medika Indonesia Healthcare Corporation announced its plan to build
a world-class international hospital in Bali to boost medical tourism in Indonesia. The newly
constructed hospital will provide cutting-edge cardiology, oncology, neurology, gastroenteric
pathology, and orthopaedics services. [6]


In addition, governments in the region are introducing lucrative policies to attract customers
from the global level. For instance, in August 2024, Brunei Darussalam invited visa-free
tourists from around 63 countries to explore tourist places there. Such policies are further
expected to boost tourism in the ASEAN region. [7]


Integrating wellness tourism into medical packages has become another major driver.
Patients seeking treatment can also engage in holistic recovery programmes, combining
traditional healing practices, spa therapies, and post-surgery care. This trend is particularly
prominent in Indonesia, where wellness tourism is growing rapidly. These drivers position the
ASEAN region as a top destination for medical tourists seeking affordable, high-quality, and
comprehensive healthcare experiences.

## **2 ASEAN Medical and Wellness Tourism Industry** **Insights**

### **2.1 Industry Ecosystem Analysis**

The ASEAN medical and wellness tourism industry has become a dynamic ecosystem driven
by a synergy of healthcare excellence, affordable treatment costs, and diverse wellness
offerings. The ecosystem encompasses hospitals, clinics, wellness centres, medical
facilitators, and government support.


Thailand, Malaysia, and Singapore serve as medical tourism hubs, offering advanced
treatments in fields like cardiology, orthopaedics, and cosmetic surgery. In the case
of Thailand, an estimated 3 million medical tourists received medical treatment and
rehabilitation in 2023. [9] For Malaysia, government-backed initiatives and accreditation
programmes, such as the Malaysia Healthcare Travel Council (MHTC), promote a safe and
reliable healthcare destination.


Wellness tourism, as a complementary sector to medical tourism, flourishes through natural
spas, retreats, and traditional healing practices, such as Indonesia’s Javanese or Thailand's
Thai massage. [10] Complementary services like travel agencies, insurance, and hospitality
providers integrate into the ecosystem to create seamless experiences for patients and
wellness seekers.


Digital health platforms further enhance accessibility, with telemedicine services connecting
international patients to local experts. Public-private Partnerships and government policies
supporting visa relaxations and marketing bolster the region’s competitiveness. This
interconnected ecosystem drives economic growth and establishes ASEAN as a global
leader in affordable, high-quality healthcare and holistic wellness experiences.

### **2.2 Industry Impact Forces**

Various forces drive growth and present challenges for the health and wellness tourism
industry. These industry impact forces reflect opportunities and pitfalls that influence
the sector's short-, medium-, and long-term prospects.


On the growth side, factors such as the low cost of medical treatments in developing
countries, increasing compliance with international standards for surgical procedures,
supportive government policies, and rising interest in health and wellness are transforming
the industry. Additionally, the growing demand for eco-friendly, sustainable practices and
technology integration is emerging as a significant trend.


Conversely, the industry faces several challenges that act as pitfalls. High costs of wellness
programmes, long wait times for specific medical procedures, and issues related to patient
follow-up and post-surgery complications pose significant hurdles. Furthermore, limited
awareness and education about wellness tourism hinder its growth potential.

capitalise on growth drivers, and mitigate industry pitfalls. The following table outlines these

often includes specialised yet expensive treatments such as detox programmes, spa

retreats in Thailand and Bali became premium offerings. These highly priced offerings include
luxury accommodations, personalised health assessments, and specialised organic meals,
but they remain inaccessible for a broad section of potential customers from neighbouring
ASEAN countries and budget-conscious tourists.


Despite efforts by ASEAN nations to promote themselves as affordable wellness

the rising cost of operations due to inflation, along with the use of premium organic products
and international wellness experts, further escalated the costs of these programmes.

combined with modern wellness treatments saw prices rise by 15%. Such prices have
placed ASEAN wellness tourism in direct competition with established European and

_**Long wait time for certain medical procedures**_


While Thailand, Singapore, and Malaysia are major hubs for medical tourism, their healthcare
systems have increasingly faced strain due to rising demand. The COVID-19 pandemic
exacerbated these delays, as healthcare resources were diverted to handling the health
crisis, reducing capacity for non-urgent medical procedures.


In Malaysia, for instance, post-pandemic medical tourism rebounded in 2022, leading
to an increase in demand for services like cosmetic surgery and fertility treatments.
This surge, coupled with a shortage of healthcare staff, has resulted in waiting times
stretching from weeks to months for hip replacements and knee surgeries.


The long wait times, especially for elective surgeries, have pushed potential medical
tourists to seek alternatives in countries with shorter waiting periods or to postpone their
treatments entirely.


Addressing these wait time challenges as the ASEAN medical tourism market grows is critical
to maintaining the region's competitive edge. Expanding healthcare infrastructure, training
more specialists, and adopting telemedicine for follow-up care could alleviate the strain
on these healthcare systems.


_**Patient follow-up and post-surgery complications**_


One of the most significant restraints affecting the ASEAN medical and wellness tourism
market is the issue around patient follow-up and post-surgery complications. Medical tourists
often return home soon after complicated medical treatments or surgeries, which presents
challenges in continuous post-surgical care and monitoring. A lack of proper follow-up can
lead to severe complications, such as infections, improper healing, or adverse reactions
to medication.


Medical tourists in ASEAN countries reported complications post-surgery due to insufficient
follow-up care. These complications are exacerbated when patients face difficulties
accessing the healthcare providers who initially treated them. This problem is particularly
apparent in popular medical tourism destinations such as Thailand and Malaysia. This can
lead to dissatisfaction and decreased confidence in medical services. Furthermore, the cost
of returning to the host country for follow-up visits is prohibitive for many patients, contributing
to them forgoing critical postoperative care.

Typical medical tourism practices can increase the risk of medical complications. A study
by Dr. Valorie A Crooks. [21] Stated that medical tourists who fly abroad are thought
to be at heightened risk for deep vein thrombosis or pulmonary embolism
owing to long-distance air travel following surgery. There is also concern that medical tourists
are at risk of exposure to blood-borne infection due to unsafe practices on blood collection,
screening and storage in destination countries.


Individuals travelling for organ transplantation, in particular, may experience higher rates
of severe infectious complications because of inadequate screening protocols. Furthermore,
medical tourism can disrupt the continuity of care and result in gaps in the documentation
of a patient’s medical history. As a result, medical tourists may lack adequate access
to follow-up once back home owing to documentation gaps.


_**Limited awareness and education about wellness tourism**_


Despite ASEAN's rich potential for medical and wellness tourism, many tourists are unaware
of the full range of these offerings. A survey conducted by the Global Wellness Institute
in 2021 revealed that only ~35% of international travellers regarded Southeast Asia
as a destination for wellness tourism. Southeast Asian nations are often associated more with
general tourism than specialised wellness services such as detox retreats, yoga resorts,
or traditional healing therapies. This lack of awareness stems from insufficient marketing
efforts, particularly in non-Asian countries.


Moreover, there is a gap in educational content targeted at international travellers about
the health benefits of wellness services offered in the ASEAN region. Many consumers in key
target markets like Europe and North America remain unfamiliar with traditional wellness
practices such as Thai massage, Balinese spa treatments, or herbal remedies, limiting their
interest in travelling to Southeast Asia for wellness experiences.


The fragmented nature of the ASEAN wellness tourism market contributes to this lack
of awareness. Unlike Europe, where countries have unified marketing campaigns promoting
wellness tourism, ASEAN lacks a cohesive regional strategy to promote its offerings. Data
from the ASEAN Tourism Strategic Plan (2016–2025) show that while some efforts have
been made to integrate health and wellness into tourism promotion, the impact has been
minimal, with wellness tourism accounting for less of a percentage point of the total tourism
revenue in key ASEAN destinations as of 2023. [22]


Furthermore, local service providers often focus on domestic clients or tourists already
familiar with their offerings, neglecting the vast international market. Without concerted efforts
to increase awareness and educate potential wellness tourists about the unique benefits
of ASEAN wellness services, the region will struggle to attract a significant share of the global
wellness tourism market.

#### **_2.2.5 Medical Coverage_**

The medical coverage scenario in the ASEAN medical tourism market presents opportunities
and challenges, significantly influencing the growth and accessibility of healthcare services
for international patients. One of the key factors driving medical tourism in the ASEAN region
is the affordability of healthcare services. However, the availability and extent of medical
coverage for patients seeking treatment in ASEAN countries vary significantly, depending on
the country of origin, insurance providers, and specific agreements between countries
or healthcare facilities.


18


For international medical tourists, private health insurance plays a crucial role in determining
the feasibility of receiving treatment in ASEAN countries. Many global insurance companies,
including Aetna International and Cignal Global, now offer specific plans covering treatments
abroad, including in popular ASEAN destinations like Thailand, Malaysia, and Singapore.
This is a key factor in the growth of medical tourism.


Despite the availability of such insurance plans, not all treatments or procedures are covered.
Elective surgeries, such as cosmetic or plastic surgeries, often require patients to pay
out of pocket, as many insurance companies categorise these procedures as non-essential.
Moreover, medical coverage for pre-existing conditions is often limited or excluded, posing
a challenge for patients with chronic illnesses seeking treatment in ASEAN countries.


Local insurance regulations and partnerships between ASEAN healthcare facilities and
international insurance providers further influence the market. Some hospitals and clinics
in Malaysia and Thailand have established direct billing agreements with foreign insurance
companies, streamlining the process for medical tourists. For example, Bumrungrad
International Hospital in Thailand partners with over 100 international insurance companies.
In contrast, other ASEAN countries, such as Viet Nam and Indonesia, are still developing
similar frameworks, which can complicate patient insurance claims. [23]


Medical tourists from countries without comprehensive international health insurance often
rely on medical travel insurance. This insurance typically covers emergency treatments
or unexpected complications during travel, but may not cover planned medical procedures.
The varying levels of coverage and complex reimbursement processes impact medical
tourists' decision-making, highlighting the need for more standardised insurance frameworks
to support the growing ASEAN medical tourism market.

### **5.9 Viet Nam**


Viet Nam’s medical and wellness tourism market is rapidly growing, driven by increasing
government investments in healthcare infrastructure and a focus on expanding medical
tourism. Key cities like Ho Chi Minh City and Hanoi are becoming medical hubs, offering
cosmetic surgery, dental care, orthopaedic treatments, and fertility services at competitive
prices compared to neighbouring countries.


Moreover, the authorities are actively involved in promotional activities to spread awareness
about medical services and products. For instance, in June 2023, the Ho Chi Minh City DoT
arranged a press conference in collaboration with the Department of Health to create
awareness about Ho Chi Minh City medical tourism products. According to the authorities'
estimates, Ho Chi Minh City can be a potential medical tourism hotspot for patients
in Northeast Asia or Europe.


The affordability of treatments, improving healthcare standards, and the presence
of internationally accredited hospitals attract a significant number of international patients,
particularly from Southeast Asia, Europe, and the U.S. In addition, Viet Nam’s medical
tourism sector is also bolstered by public-private partnerships, which enhance service quality
and expand the range of available treatments. For instance, in January 2024, the Thomson
Medical Group announced the acquisition of FV Medical to consolidate its presence
in the Viet Nam market. The Thomson Medical Group also plans to become the leading
hospital in the Indo-China region. [85]


Wellness tourism in Viet Nam is also on the rise, supported by the country’s rich natural
landscapes, including beaches, mountains, and countryside, which provide an ideal setting
for relaxation and rejuvenation. In addition, the government is also promoting Viet Nam

the Ho Chi Minh City DoT arranged the 18th International Travel Expo Ho Chi Minh City 2024
to provide a platform for investors and market players to discuss sustainable tourism and
hospitality business models and innovative product discussions. The event was organised
as a part of Viet Nam's international tourism expansion strategy.


Traditional Vietnamese healing practices, such as herbal medicine, acupuncture, and
therapeutic massages, are integrated into wellness programmes, drawing tourists who seek
holistic health experiences. Destinations like Da Nang, Nha Trang, and Phu Quoc are popular
for their luxury wellness resorts, which offer detox programmes, yoga, and meditation
in tranquil settings.
