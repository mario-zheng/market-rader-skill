**Introduction**


There is no doubt that 2025 is shaping up to be a

year of huge transformation for government agencies

across the globe. This report presents the findings of
our studies into the customer and employee experiences
with government services, identifying three critical

areas of strategic focus for the year 2025:
Trust, Productivity, and Resilience.


The efficacy of governmental service delivery is

fundamentally predicated upon public trust.
And employees—those engaged in direct service

interactions and those responsible for policy, program
design, and operational functions—need organizational
trust to perform at their best.


The imperative for enhanced productivity within

government is being accelerated by persistent change,
fiscal constraints, and efficiency initiatives. This pressure

extends beyond employee engagement—it directly
impacts citizen expectations. Customers are looking
to get what they need, when they want it, the first
time they seek it.


2 // Qualtrics 2025 Government Trends Report // Introduction



Resilience in the face of unexpected challenges has
emerged as a core competency for government agencies.
Customers affected by natural disasters, cybersecurity
breaches, or pandemics, rely upon governmental support
for recovery. For government employees, disruption such

as austerity measures and other fast-moving initiatives
to increase government efficiency, or the slower erosion

of resources while expectations increase, need resilience
to maintain operational continuity. By keeping human

experience at the center of their choices, government
leaders can deliver trust, productivity, and resilience for
their customers and employees.

the first place? Employees need support

carries this burden.

leaders need to look for _more ways to_

_understand_ what customers are actually
experiencing, in order to surface the
insights they need to drive positive
change. Doing so will result not only in
improved outcomes for customers, but
a more engaged workforce that delivers
better experiences for those they serve.

**Let’s not forget AI…**
**and how governments**
**are slower to adopt it**


_Despite the promise of AI to increase efficiency_ and
free government employees from mundane tasks,

governments around the world are lagging behind

a potential security risk for government systems. This
is particularly concerning when less than one third of
government employees report being enabled on AI
principles, guidance and ethics—compared to 52% of
employees from all other industries.

**Definitions**


**Engagement:** Employees possess the physical, emotional,
and psychological resources necessary to invest fully in their roles.
Employers care about Engagement because it predicts future
organizational performance. The more engaged an individual,
the higher their discretionary effort. Engagement is persistent;

it takes more than one bad or good day to change how engaged
someone is in their work. Rather, it is driven by the cumulative
effect of day-to-day Employee Experiences.


_Recommend:_ Likelihood to recommend the organization
as a place to work.


_Personal Accomplishment:_ The employee’s connection to
their job and work; their sense of purpose.


_Motivation:_ Willingness to go above and beyond.


25 // Qualtrics 2025 Government Trends Report // Definitions



**Expectations Exceeded:** Employees believe their organization is
living up to their expectations about their experience. This item is
distinct from Engagement and can be thought of as a broader
measure that encapsulates elements of the psychological contract.
It also acknowledges that expectations shift as people navigate
different points in their employee journey, the distinct changes in
needs and desires through the phases of life, and evolving norms in
the world around them.


**Intent to Stay 3 or more years:** This KPI is a measure of overall
organizational commitment, and has been empirically linked to actual
turnover. Because replacing employees is costly for organizations, intent
to stay is an important predictive measure of workforce churn.



**Inclusion:** Organizations create a sense of inclusion
when they see diverse identities as a source of insight, and offer

employees opportunities to leverage their unique skills and attributes.
Qualtrics’ measure of inclusion is made up of three components:


_Equity_ means equal outcomes for different groups of employees in the
workplace. Equitable workplaces make adjustments to ensure that all

employees have fair and equal access to opportunity.


_Belonging_ is a key and distinct psychological construct. Employees
who have a positive connection with their workplace and colleagues are

more engaged, productive and resilient.


_Authenticity_ is feeling like you can be yourself. Being integrated into an
organization without feeling that you be yourself creates discomfort,
and demands cognitive energy from employees who are required to
adapt or assimilate.


**Wellbeing:** Individuals have what they need to ensure positive
working habits. Drawing from the latest research, Qualtrics measure

of well-being has three components:


_Trusting Relationships:_ Social connections and meaningful

relationships at work enable employees to successfully
navigate challenges they face.


_Self-Positivity:_ A good baseline level of self-esteem or self-confidence is
important to overall employee well-being . When we feel positive about
ourselves we tend to feel better about life in general.


_Energized:_ Employees who have high levels of well-being feel energized
at work. This can also be a good indicator of potential burnout.

**Government Customer Experience:** Government organizations
seeking to achieve positive results for their customers focus on
two top-level KPIs: Satisfaction and Trust. While commercial

organizations also look at customers’ likelihood to recommend the
service, and willingness to purchase again, these are not relevant for
government interactions which are often mandatory.


_Satisfaction:_ Customers are satisfied when interactions with
a government service are easy and they successfully achieve
their goals. The emotional experience customers have with the

interaction is consistently lower for governments than for other
industries—when customers are unsatisfied, or cannot achieve
their goals, the cost to serve them only increases and this creates

an exponential drain on the organization’s resources as they
fix broken experiences.


_Trust:_ Trust is an important indicator of customers’ experiences

with Government organizations. If customers do not trust their

government, they are less likely to adopt public policies
designed to protect and promote their welfare. Their distrust
could also cause disruption that jeopardizes the government’s
ability to sustain operations.
