### **Letter from** **the CEO**

**For more than a century, Mars has operated by taking**
**the long view. That approach has never felt more**
**consequential than it does today.**


The world our business operates in, and depends on,
continues to change. Conflict, economic shocks and extreme
weather are reshaping how we operate. What was once
exceptional is becoming routine, from supply chain disruption
to changing growing seasons.



That is exactly why sustainability is central to how we grow
and thrive. Not only because it’s the right thing to do for
people and the planet, but because it’s the only way to build
resilience and economic certainty for our business and the
thousands of people across our full value chain.


It is about generating good jobs, strengthening the livelihoods
of farmers, creating energy security through investment in
clean and affordable electricity and building factories and
veterinary hospitals with the smallest environmental footprint
and significant positive social impact.


Resilience is critical to business continuity and to ensuring we
are here for at least another 100 years. As a family-owned,
privately held company, the ability to think in generations
rather than quarters is not an aspiration — it is embedded
in how we operate. At Mars, financial goals and societal
responsibilities go hand in hand across the business. This is
evident in everything from M&A to long-term remuneration
strategy, where incentives for approximately 1,900 managers
across Mars are partly tied to sustainability goals. Our focus
is on the impact we have today and the legacy we leave for
future generations.


In 2025, we invested in upgrading and expanding our
factories, helping us serve more consumers and strengthen
our operations. At the same time, we continued making
meaningful progress against our sustainability ambitions
in an increasingly challenging environment. We reduced
absolute carbon emissions by 6.4% [1] across our full value chain,
delivering an accumulated 2025 result of -16.9% versus our
2015 baseline, while growing the business by approximately
75%. This reduction – our biggest decarbonization year to
date – was driven primarily by reducing raw material carbon
intensity and by our factory decarbonization program.



In 2025, we gave a major push to our Renewables Acceleration
(RAcc) program, which is starting to expand the generation
of renewable electricity to cover the needs of our full value
chain — from farms and transport to the energy consumers
use at home. By 2030, this effort could potentially reduce
emissions by roughly 3 Mt, or approximately 10% of our current
footprint. By bringing significantly greater demand to the
market in a single, coordinated approach, RAcc delivers a faster
and more efficient pathway to scaling renewable energy than
traditional supplier engagement alone. These are the kind of
breakthroughs that give me hope for the future.


Addressing our wider impact also requires close collaboration
with farmers and investment in climate-smart agriculture [2] that
delivers benefits for both people and the planet. In 2025, we
supported approximately 77 climate-smart agriculture projects
across 26 countries and 12 crops. This work is designed to
deliver both environmental and economic outcomes, helping
strengthen resilience on farms while supporting more stable
livelihoods. In the end, if this work is to scale it must also make
economic sense.


In packaging, we continue to invest hundreds of millions of
dollars redesigning our portfolio to better align with local
recycling systems, while increasing recycled content.
We are also advancing progress across our veterinary clinics
and hospitals, including pilots to reduce plastic waste and keep
materials out of landfills. In 2025, the program diverted 4.3
metric tons of plastic from landfills.

Our ambitions are also about improving everyday wellbeing
for people and pets and using our scale to expand access to

In a complex and shifting landscape, we continue to grow
and invest in our portfolio. We are now a highly diversified
business with approximately 170,000 Associates. In December
2025, we completed the acquisition of Kellanova, our biggest

communities and the planet we rely on.


I am incredibly proud of our progress. However, global supply
chain complexity alongside climate, agricultural and socioeconomic pressures, present real risks to the systems we all
depend on.


We need to acknowledge that no single company can drive
the required level of systemic change alone. It requires longterm collaboration across the full value chain — with farmers,
suppliers, partners, policymakers, peers and others — to
continue making progress in a meaningful way.


**What gives me confidence is the commitment of**
**our Associates, the strength of our brands and our**
**continued ability to adapt, invest and innovate for the**
**long term. I look forward to continuing to bring joy,**
**nutrition and support to people and pets all over the**
**world. We will continue our efforts to generate good**
**jobs and support our Associates and communities,**
**while continuing to make progress towards a more**
**resilient and more sustainable future for all.**


Poul Weihrauch
CEO & Office of the President,

### **Embedding** **Sustainability** **Across the** **Business**

Embedding sustainability across a global business
requires integration into everyday processes
and decisions. At Mars, we focus on building the
capabilities, tools and accountability needed to
make sustainability part of everyone’s job.



**Governance and Business Planning**


Sustainability is already integrated into many core governance
and business planning processes at Mars, and we continue to
expand the scope of this work.


Across our business, sustainability is integrated into financial
planning and activity management, supported by functional
experts. We redesigned the sustainability components
of both our five-year Integrated Value Creation Plan and
our annual business planning process to introduce more
tightly integrated annual budgeting and sustainability
performance planning.


Additionally, we are building new capabilities to better
assess, prioritize and manage cross-impact strategies.
To support this, we have formalized a new Sustainable
Performance Acceleration team that focuses on strengthening
how we deliver progress and embed sustainability into
decision-making.


Sustainability is also embedded in how we manage growth
and transformation. Our mergers and acquisitions (M&A)
approach includes assessing the environmental and societal
footprint of potential acquisitions, followed by integration
into our plans (and consequent reporting) to address impacts
over time. This approach is reflected in our recent acquisition
of Kellanova. After considering sustainability impacts from
the outset, we are now integrating the business, aligning it
with broader business priorities and assessing it for overall
impact and performance. The footprint of the newly acquired
business and its brands is being incorporated into Mars
sustainability reporting throughout 2026.



**Data and Digital Tools**


Delivering meaningful progress on sustainability requires
thousands of decisions at every level of the organization.
Access to accurate, granular sustainability data enables better
decision-making, and we are expanding access so more teams
can understand and act on their role in delivering it.


In 2025, Mars continued expanding our “Mars Sustainability
Mission Control”, an ecosystem of applications and data assets
that improves tracking, transparency and decision-making
across operations and supply chains. A key enhancement was
providing access to human rights risk data and processes,
allowing our teams to screen for risks by country and
commodity and create actionable plans for managing and
addressing those risks, including through integration into
their sourcing strategies.


**Leadership Accountability**


Leadership accountability reinforces the integration of
sustainability across the business. Approximately 1,900
senior leaders’ long-term compensation is tied to nonfinancial sustainability metrics such as greenhouse gas (GHG)
reductions, helping ensure that sustainability performance is
embedded into leadership incentives and decision-making.

### **Investing in** **Sustainability**

**Consistent investment in people, operations and**
**systems is needed to deliver progress over time,**
**including targeted investments to accelerate**
**innovation where solutions are not currently available.**


In 2025, Mars announced the Mars Sustainability Investment
Fund (MSIF). With a total capital commitment of up to
$250 million, the fund invests in areas such as:


→ Sustainable agriculture: technologies that reduce

emissions associated with agricultural inputs in
our products


→ Novel ingredients and raw materials: lower emissions

or “better-for-you” alternatives to existing ingredients
used in our products


→ Sustainable packaging: designed for circularity with a

focus on recyclable, compostable and other alternative
replacements for flexible plastics



The fund aims to invest in companies developing cuttingedge solutions that address key industry sustainability
challenges: lower-emissions production, more resilient
supply chains and packaging solutions better aligned with
a circular economy.


While outcomes take time and conditions continue to evolve,
we view these investments as an important part of building
the capabilities, ventures and innovations needed to advance
our SiGP.


In addition to the Mars Sustainability Investment Fund, we
created the Mars Impact Fund in late 2025 to accelerate our
commitment to creating a better tomorrow and complement
our existing sustainability and philanthropic efforts.


Backed by a significant, long-term financial commitment,
the Fund makes strategic philanthropic investments
designed to deliver meaningful and lasting impact. Guided
by a partnership-led approach, we focus on strengthening
sourcing community resilience, expanding the pipeline of
scientists and improving companion animal wellbeing in
the communities where we operate.



**Mars Climate School —**
**Building Climate Capability**
**Across the Organization**


In 2025, Mars launched Mars Climate School, a
comprehensive e-learning series designed to build
climate literacy across the organization. The program is
required for senior leaders and Associates in functions
critical to delivering the Net Zero Roadmap. For
all other Associates, it is optional but encouraged.
Climate School includes three modules: Climate Basics,

By strengthening internal climate capability, Mars aims
to equip its workforce to support delivery against
its Net Zero ambition while reinforcing long-term
business resilience.

### **Climate Action**

**At Mars, we remain focused on creating meaningful impact across our entire value**
**chain, building on years of progress in reducing our GHG emissions. This experience**
**[has informed the development of our Net Zero Roadmap, which sets out an](https://www.mars.com/sites/g/files/dfsbuz106/files/2023-09/Mars Net Zero Roadmap 2050_2.pdf)**
**ambitious and practical path to achieve Net Zero by 2050.**


Our full value-chain GHG reduction targets were first established in 2017 and our emissions
peaked in 2018, against a 2015 baseline. [13] In 2025, we achieved our largest annual absolute GHG
reduction to date at 6.4%. We have delivered our Science Based Target to reduce Scope 1 and
Scope 2 GHGs, with a 42.6% reduction compared to 2015. We reduced our total GHG emissions
by approximately 16.9% since 2015. In 2017, before developing our Net Zero Roadmap, we set a
Science Based Target of 27% GHG reduction across our full value chain by 2025. Since then, the
business footprint has changed and the data and science available has evolved, which required us
[to recalculate our footprint according to our GHG Baseline Restatements Policy. Our business has](https://www.mars.com/about/policies-and-practices/ghg-baseline-restatement-policy)
expanded significantly due to acquisitions, and we have made updates to our land use change
accounting. Integrating these changes in a transparent and diligent way means we did not hit
the 2025 target we set out to achieve. However, we have continued to decouple growth from
emissions, which means that our efforts are working. We continue to deliver strong absolute
GHG reductions year on year.


Our GHG reductions across Scopes 1, 2 and 3 have been largely driven by expanded climatesmart agriculture and deforestation-free supply chain efforts in our value chain, sourcing and
recipe optimization and rapid acceleration of renewable electricity for our factories and value
chain. In 2025, our GHG footprint includes 48,624 metric tons of GHG removals in climate-smart
agriculture projects associated with our value chain.

**Coral Reef Restoration**

Coral reefs are among the world’s most biodiverse and
productive ecosystems, supporting approximately 30% of
marine life while covering less than 1% of the ocean floor. Yet
around 50% of the world’s coral reefs have been lost or severely
degraded, with four global mass coral bleaching events
recorded in recent decades putting the health of our oceans
and the livelihoods of hundreds of millions of people at risk.


Recognizing that many reefs can no longer recover naturally,
Mars Sustainable Solutions (MSS) is working with partners to
advance science-led coral restoration and build local capacity
to support long-term ocean health.


MSS coral restoration program has been active for 20 years and
combines scientific research with community-based training to
deliver coral reef restoration at ecologically meaningful scales
worldwide. To date, our reef restoration efforts have supported
projects across 17 countries and 124 restoration sites.


In 2025, the program expanded into four new countries and 28
new restoration sites, while training approximately 67 new reef
builders in coral restoration techniques. In addition, the MSS
team increased its global training capacity by adding six new
global trainers, bringing the total to 27, aiming to strengthen
both local and global restoration capability.


By transferring knowledge and strengthening local capacity,
our coral restoration program continues to expand its
global footprint, enabling reef restoration at scales that are
meaningful for both ecosystems and the communities that
depend on them.



**SHEBA** **[®]** **Hope Grows™ —**
**Supporting Coastal Communities**


Launched in 2021, The SHEBA [®] Hope Grows™ program
brings our coral reef restoration efforts to life by
combining science-based restoration with communityled action. Working alongside local NGOs, scientists
and community members, the program supports the
restoration of damaged coral reefs while building
skills, livelihoods and long-term stewardship in
coastal communities.


Using the Mars Assisted Reef Restoration System
(MARRS), the program has supported the deployment
of 7,450 Sheba-funded Reef Stars across 10 locations.
At one Sheba-funded reef in Indonesia, internal
monitoring data from 2025 reveals significant
ecological improvements: an 85% increase in fish
population, a 21% rise in fish biomass, a 17% growth
in hard coral cover and a 68% reduction in rubble.
The program amplifies its impact through storytelling
initiatives like the Amazon Prime Reef Builders
documentary and interactive digital experiences,
engaging new audiences and highlighting the
partnerships driving change.

**Palm Positive Plan — Advancing**
**Responsible Recruitment**

employers to bear the costs of any recruitment fees. As
part of our Palm Positive Plan, Mars has been working

broader industry.

recruitment-related fees repaid to 2,944 migrant

systems to help prevent recurrence.

additional suppliers, embedding stronger safeguards
in our palm supply chain. Mars representatives sit on
the RSPO Standards Review Task Force, where we have
shared our experience implementing these flagship
responsible recruitment initiatives and advocated for
the 2024 update of RSPO standards related to forced
labor. In 2025, Mars co-authored RSPO’s Guidance for
the Repayment of Recruitment Fees and Related Costs,
which provides practical guidance to companies.

*** Definition of “Mass Balance”**

In certain markets, Mars may purchase and use ingredients (e.g., cocoa
butter; palm oil; etc.) in its products which a supplier has purchased,
processed, shipped or stored the ingredients, or the raw material used
to make ingredients (e.g., cocoa beans; fruit of palm trees; etc.), on a
“mass balance” basis. Suppliers who purchase, process, ship or store
ingredients, or the raw materials used to make ingredients, on a “mass
balance” basis frequently mix conventional, deforestation free and other
types of ingredients or their raw materials at one or more steps in their
supply chain. As a result, Mars cannot identify whether a specific product
has been made from conventional, deforestation free or another type
of ingredient or raw material or a mixture of two or more types of an
ingredient or raw material.

Informed by the AFi – a leading collective of organizations providing
comprehensive, consensus-based guidance to eliminate deforestation,
conversion and human rights abuses from agricultural and forestry supply
chains – Mars has established DCF ambition, principles and targets. AFi’s
Operational Guidance on Commitments and Progress Pathways (2025)
describes fulfilling a DCF commitment as reaching a steady state of high
performance – as opposed to being a one-time achievement – in which
all or nearly all materials in the company’s supply chain are DCF at any
given time and systems are in place to maintain high performance over
time. This definition of fulfilling a DCF commitment takes a practical
and inclusive approach, accounts for business dynamics and allows for
prioritization based on risk.



Disclaimer and definition of achievement and
maintenance of DCF supply chain commitment



For purposes of DCF progress and claims, Mars considers a commodity’s
commitment to be achieved and maintained when at least 95% of in-scope
volumes meet Mars’ DCF Principles and DCF Standard and the remaining
<5% meets one of the following criteria


- Non-compliant volumes sourced from suppliers exiting Mars’ supply
chain within 18 months of the first date that Mars becomes aware of
the non-compliance. After the 18-month grace period, these volumes
will no longer qualify for the <5% threshold if they remain in the Mars
supply chain.

- Non-compliant volumes sourced from suppliers actively working
towards DCF compliance for volumes Mars sources so long as a
corrective action plan is in place and progress toward remediation has
been demonstrated within 18 months of the first date Mars becomes
aware of the non-compliance. After the 18-month grace period, the
previously excluded non-compliant volumes will no longer qualify for
the <5% threshold if they remain in the Mars supply chain.


Volumes of unknown status or non-compliant volumes for which DCF
compliance is not possible or reasonably practical. Volumes falling within
this exception must be approved by Mars’ Corporate Sustainability Team
and re-evaluated at least once annually to determine whether DCF
compliance has become feasible and/or there remains a valid basis to
retain the non-compliant volumes.
